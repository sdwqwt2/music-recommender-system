\begin{abstract}
\noindent In music streaming platforms, up to 35\% of user interaction logs correspond to immediate skips ($<30$ seconds), reflecting user dissatisfaction. Standard Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) penalizes causal autoregressive transformers for failing to predict such negative interactions (the \textit{False Negative} dilemma), inadvertently amplifying disliked content. In this work, we investigate the alignment of a Causal GPT-2 sequential recommender (43.2M parameters) with true user satisfaction using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) anchored with auxiliary SFT regularization ($\beta=0.2, \gamma=0.10$). Extensive evaluations on the industrial-scale Yandex Music dataset (\textbf{Yambda-50M}: 98,505 catalog tokens, 33.2M training interactions, 9,052 held-out test users) demonstrate that DPO not only outperforms 2-stage Critic Re-Ranking and Actor-Critic PPO, but completely circumvents the alignment tax (\textit{Zero Alignment Tax}). The aligned DPO policy achieves statistically significant ranking order improvements in \textbf{NDCG@10} ($0.1290 \to 0.1307, \mathbf{p = 0.0366}$) and \textbf{MRR} ($0.1123 \to 0.1142, \mathbf{p = 0.0260}$), alongside next-item retrieval gains (\textbf{19.37\% $\to$ 19.52\% HR@10}, net $+14$ users), expands catalog coverage by \textbf{+197 unique tracks} from the long tail, reduces popularity Spearman rank correlation by \textbf{$-13.4\%$}, and yields a \textbf{+2.1s} session duration lift in 5-step autoregressive rollouts without exposure bias degradation.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Sequential Recommendation, Reinforcement Learning Alignment, Direct Preference Optimization, Causal GPT-2, Music Streaming, Skip Dilemma, Zero Alignment Tax, Catalog Diversity, Yambda.
\end{abstract}
